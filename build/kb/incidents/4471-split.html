parser: day and month change places

The shared parser reads every date month first. Every tenant on file writes the day first. So a date the tenant writes as 03/10 comes out as 10 March: “Customer booked 3 October and got an invite for 10 March.” This is the problem #4471 describes, and the queued fix is for it.

hint: the booking-v3 form asks for the month first

booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People at day-first tenants on booking-v3 type the month first, as the hint tells them, and the form reads the date day first. The report looks the same as a parser report. Only the tenant’s form and the channel (form) tell them apart. A parser fix does not fix it, because the input itself is in the wrong order.

one-day-move: not a swap

Some reports describe a date one day off, or two recipients who see different days for the same meeting: “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday. Same meeting. We held it twice.” A day/month swap cannot do that. The desk already noted these reports in desk-notes.json, and the cause table on #4471 files them under parser, because it sorts by tenant. This page counts them the same way: under their cause, and again in their own row. The cause is not known yet. The ticket asks engineering to check time zones and daylight saving time.

For the desk

  • Intake’s tag is a guess from the reporter’s browser. The tagged as column shows that it disagrees with the tenant. Place a report by its tenant, its channel and its body, not by its tag.
  • A subject line tells you what a person saw, not why it happened. Here every problem in the table shares the same “wrong date” subjects. Decision 0001 accepted that (“engineering merges them later”), but a ticket marked known hid the other problems behind the fix for the parser. Split a big ticket by cause before you mark it known.
